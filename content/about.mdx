I came to engineering from the design side. After a few years in art direction, translating brand mockups into browser-ready layouts, I realized I wanted to build the underlying systems instead of just painting them.

Over the last ten years, I've shipped production software across government platforms, HR SaaS, and high-traffic product studios. Frontend is where I spend most of my time, with TypeScript and React as my defaults. On the backend, I write Node.js and Python. I pick based on the team constraints and the problem, rather than preference.

The art direction background doesn't surface in pull requests, but it shapes how I evaluate an interface. It's why I care about holding 60fps on mid-range Android phones just as much as cutting Next.js hydration times. I've found that performance bottlenecks are almost always design problems that got deferred until they became someone else's emergency.

Lately, I've been writing more Python to work with applied ML. Building RAG pipelines and experimenting with LoRA fine-tuning changes how you architect the application layer around these models. You make better product decisions when you actually understand what the tools do under the hood.

I relocated from Brazil two years ago and am now permanently based in Vietnam. Building from Southeast Asia puts you closer to how a massive part of the world actually uses the web. Hardware constraints and slow connectivity aren't edge cases here. They are the baseline.

I care about systems that the next person can understand without a guided tour. I also think pair programming makes both the code and the people in the room better. If you need someone who connects clean architecture directly to product thinking, I'm usually worth the conversation.
